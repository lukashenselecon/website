Peking University · Guanghua School of Management

I am an Associate Professor of Economics at the Guanghua School of Management, Peking University, a J-PAL Invited Researcher, and an IZA Research Fellow. My research is about how people find work, stay in it, and cope when they lose it.

I run field experiments and large surveys, usually together with governments, firms, and NGOs, to study three things: how jobseekers search and how employers hire, how workers and their families absorb the shock of losing a job and how severance pay can soften it, and how workplace conditions, from the quality of managers to working hours and job security, shape careers and wellbeing.

A second strand of my work looks at political participation and how beliefs about peers and opponents shape it.

Most of my fieldwork is in Ethiopia, South Africa, Kyrgyzstan, China, and Vietnam.

Published articles are on Publications; drafts and field work still under way are on Work in Progress.
